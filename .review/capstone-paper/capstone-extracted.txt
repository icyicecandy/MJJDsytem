[P0152][Normal] Abstract:       This study developed a web-based Customer Relationship Management System for Mary Joy and Jepsy Dave Telecom to address difficulties in customer application processing, record management, installation coordination, and customer support communication. Using a quantitative descriptive-developmental approach and Agile Software Development Methodology, the study identified existing operational methods, problems, functional requirements, and preferred system features. Messaging applications or online platforms ranked first among the existing methods, while delays in responding to customer concerns ranked first among the problems encountered. Live chat or direct customer support ranked first among the functional requirements, while real-time status tracking ranked first among the preferred system features. The developed system centralized customer applications, records, technician assignments, installation monitoring, ticketing, live chat, notifications, and public tracking. The study supports Sustainable Development Goal 9 on Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure. The system obtained an overall average weighted mean of 4.3, interpreted as Strongly Agree.

[P0368][Normal] CHAPTER I
[P0369][Normal] INTRODUCTION
[P0370][Normal] Background of the Study
[P0371][Normal] Internet connectivity had become an essential infrastructure that supported communication, education, commerce, and public services worldwide. As dependence on digital connectivity continued to grow, Internet Service Providers (ISPs) were expected to manage not only network performance but also customer interactions and service processes efficiently. The increasing number of internet users placed greater pressure on telecommunications companies to maintain organized customer records, respond promptly to service concerns, and coordinate operational activities effectively. When customer information, service requests, and technical coordination were handled through fragmented or manual processes, operational inefficiencies often arose. Studies showed that effective customer relationship management (CRM) systems could improve organizational efficiency by centralizing customer data, facilitating communication, and supporting better service delivery within telecommunications organizations (Ali & Ahmad, 2024; Fernando et al., 2023). These systems helped service providers organize customer interactions, monitor service activities, and improve overall customer satisfaction.
[P0372][Normal] In the telecommunications industry, service quality and customer relationship management played a crucial role in maintaining long-term customer relationships. Research emphasized that telecommunications providers needed to adopt structured systems to manage customer data, service requests, and operational workflows in order to ensure service reliability and responsiveness. For example, in the study “The Impact of Electronic Customer Relationship Management Strategies on Customer Loyalty: A Mediated Model” by Magatef et al. (2023), the authors found that electronic customer relationship management strategies in telecommunications significantly improved customer relationship quality, which in turn positively influenced customer loyalty. Their findings supported the idea that digital CRM systems were effective because they improved communication and helped service providers manage customer interactions in a more organized and responsive manner. Likewise, the study “The Relationship between CRM, Knowledge Management, Organization Commitment, Customer Profitability and Customer Loyalty in Telecommunication Industry: The Mediating Role of Customer Satisfaction and the Moderating Role of Brand Image” by Gazi et al. (2024) showed that effective CRM in the telecommunications industry had a strong positive effect on customer satisfaction, profitability, and loyalty. The study further showed that better CRM performance contributed both directly and indirectly to stronger customer retention through improved customer satisfaction. These findings indicated that when customer data and service operations were poorly organized, telecommunications providers could experience delayed responses, inconsistent service, and difficulty monitoring performance. However, when structured digital CRM approaches were implemented, they could improve service coordination, responsiveness, and long-term customer relationships.
[P0373][Normal] In the Philippines, the demand for reliable internet services had increased significantly because of the rapid expansion of digital communication, online learning, remote work, and electronic commerce. National digitalization initiatives further emphasized the importance of expanding internet access and improving service quality across the country. According to the Philippine Development Plan 2023–2028, digital transformation played a vital role in improving economic productivity and expanding access to services (National Economic and Development Authority [NEDA], 2023). Similarly, the Philippine Statistics Authority (2023) reported the continued growth of the digital economy as internet usage expanded across sectors. These developments highlighted the growing importance of internet service providers in supporting the country’s digital infrastructure. As internet connectivity became more integrated into everyday life, customers increasingly expected reliable service, prompt responses to concerns, and organized communication with service providers.
[P0374][Normal] Despite these developments, many internet service providers in the Philippines still faced operational challenges related to service management and customer interaction. Studies in the Philippine telecommunications sector indicated that service reliability, responsiveness, and effective communication significantly influenced how customers evaluated their service providers. However, smaller and provincial ISPs often operated with limited administrative resources and continued to rely on semi-manual methods such as spreadsheets, messaging platforms, and paper-based documentation to manage daily operations. Although these methods could be manageable on a small scale, they often resulted in fragmented information management as the number of customers increased. When customer data, service requests, and operational coordination were handled across multiple platforms, it became more difficult to maintain consistent records, monitor service activities, and respond to customer concerns in a timely manner.
[P0375][Normal] This situation was particularly relevant to small provincial internet service providers that served local communities without integrated information systems. Many of these providers managed customer applications, maintained subscriber records, coordinated installations, and addressed service concerns through separate communication channels and manually maintained files. As operations expanded, these practices could lead to incomplete documentation, difficulty retrieving customer records, delayed responses to service concerns, and challenges in coordinating operational tasks among staff. These issues underscored the need for improved service management practices that could support the increasing operational demands of local internet service providers.
[P0376][Normal] One example of this situation was observed in MJJD TELECOM, a provincial internet service provider operating in Bayambang, Pangasinan. As the number of customers increased, the company experienced difficulties in organizing customer information, monitoring service activities, and coordinating operational tasks. Customer applications and inquiries were often received through messaging platforms or physical documents, while records were maintained using spreadsheet files and other manual methods. Although these approaches enabled the company to continue its operations, they also created challenges in maintaining consistent documentation, tracking service-related activities, and retrieving customer information efficiently. These operational limitations could lead to delays in processing requests, inconsistencies in handling service concerns, and difficulty maintaining organized records as the company expanded its services.
[P0377][Normal] Although previous studies examined customer relationship management, service quality, and customer satisfaction in telecommunications, most of these studies focused on large telecommunications companies or general telecommunications settings. Limited literature specifically addressed the operational realities of small provincial internet service providers, particularly in terms of managing customer applications, installation coordination, customer records, and service concerns through an integrated digital system. Because of this, the specific operational challenges faced by smaller ISPs in provincial areas remained insufficiently explored. This gap justified the need for a study that developed a practical and localized web-based system for MJJD TELECOM.
[P0378][Normal] To address these concerns, this study introduced a web-based Customer Relationship Management System for MJJD TELECOM. The system was designed to provide a centralized platform for organizing customer records, managing service-related activities, and improving coordination among operational processes. By centralizing information and improving workflow management, the system was expected to help the organization maintain organized records, monitor service activities more effectively, and improve communication between the service provider and its customers.
[P0379][Normal] Conceptual Framework
[P0380][Normal] This study was anchored on two complementary recent studies from the telecommunications field. The first was the study of Magatef et al. (2023) entitled The Impact of Electronic Customer Relationship Management Strategies on Customer Loyalty: A Mediated Model. Their study examined electronic customer relationship management (e-CRM) strategies in the telecommunications sector and explained that e-CRM consisted of functional and personal dimensions that influenced customer loyalty through customer relationship quality. The study emphasized that structured digital interaction and electronic service platforms strengthened the relationship between service providers and customers. This was relevant to the present study because the proposed system for MJJD TELECOM included customer-centered digital functions such as online application submission, customer record management, ticketing, live chat, and public status tracking. These features reflected the functional dimension of e-CRM because they enabled the company to manage customer information and interactions through a centralized web-based platform.
[P0381][Normal] The second anchor was the study of Bandali et al. (2025) entitled ML-Based Fault Management Automation in Large-Scale Fixed and Mobile Telecommunication Networks. The study discussed how telecommunications service problems were handled through trouble tickets and showed that structured ticket-based processing was important for improving operational efficiency, resource allocation, and customer satisfaction. It also addressed the prediction of resolution time and dispatch relevance in fault-management operations. This study was relevant to the present research because the proposed system for MJJD TELECOM included service-related functions such as ticket submission, technician assignment, installation monitoring, and service status tracking. Although the present study was intended for a small provincial internet service provider rather than a large telecommunications operator, the operational principle remained similar: service requests and concerns needed to be properly documented, tracked, assigned, and resolved through an organized digital process.
[P0382][Normal] Guided by these two studies, the present research combined customer relationship management and service management into one web-based system for MJJD TELECOM. The study of Magatef et al. (2023) supported the customer relationship dimension of the framework, particularly digital interaction, organized communication, and centralized customer records, while the study of Bandali et al. (2025) supported the service operations dimension, particularly structured ticket handling, technician-related workflows, and service monitoring. At the center of the framework was a centralized web-based database that stored customer profiles, application records, installation details, ticket histories, technician assignments, and communication logs in one integrated platform. Through this setup, customer information and service activities could be accessed, updated, and monitored more efficiently.

[P0407][Normal] Scope and Delimitations
[P0408][Normal] This study focused on the design, development, and evaluation of a Customer Relationship Management System for MJJD TELECOM (Mary Joy & Jepsy Dave Telecom), a provincial internet service provider located in Bayambang, Pangasinan, that operated prepaid fiber internet services through Surf2Sawa. The proposed system centralized the key operational processes of MJJD TELECOM into a web-based platform, particularly customer application processing, installation management, scheduling, and customer communication.
[P0409][Normal] The system allowed customers to create an account, select a service plan, submit an installation application, upload a valid identification document, provide their address, and pin their exact location on a map. The administrator reviewed each application, manually checked serviceability and slot availability based on the submitted location, and either approved or rejected the request. If approved, the administrator assigned a technician and scheduled the installation. If rejected, the customer could submit a new application. The system also allowed technicians to view assigned installations, update installation statuses, and maintain installation histories. In this study, compatibility refers to the system functioning properly across commonly used browsers/devices for administrators, technicians, and customers.

[P0460][Normal] Customer Communication. [P0461][Normal] Customer Relationship Management (CRM). In this study, CRM refers to the system functions that manage customer profiles, application records, service history, and interaction logs to support organized customer handling.
[P0462][Normal] Functional Requirements. Functional requirements define what a system must do its services, behaviors, and functions needed by users (ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018). [P0463][Normal] Installation Management. Installation management refers to organizing and monitoring deployment activities to ensure completion and documentation of service installation tasks (Benzaid & Taleb, 2022). In this study, it refers to technician assignment, scheduling, status updates, and proof-of-installation uploads handled through the system.
[P0464][Normal] ISO/IEC 25010. ISO/IEC 25010 provides a product quality model defining characteristics for software quality evaluation (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, it is the standard used to evaluate the developed system.

[P0467][Normal] Portability. Portability refers to how effectively a system can be transferred from one environment to another (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, it refers to the system’s ability to be deployed across different machines/hosting environments.
[P0468][Normal] Reliability. Reliability refers to the ability of a system to perform specified functions under specified conditions for a specified period (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, it refers to stable system operation during key processes such as approvals, updates, and record saving.
[P0469][Normal] Security. Security refers to protecting information so access is appropriate to authorization (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, it refers to authenticated access and protection of uploaded IDs, application documents and customer records.
[P0470][Normal] System Features. System features refer to functions and capabilities provided to users to meet requirements and support tasks (ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018). In this study, system features refer to the modules implemented in the platform (application portal, admin dashboard, technician updates, scheduling, ticketing/chat, tracking).
[P0471][Normal] Usability. Usability refers to the degree to which specified users can use a system to achieve goals effectively, efficiently, and with satisfaction (ISO/IEC, 2023).In this study, it refers to ease of use for admins, technicians, and customers when navigating and completing tasks.

[P0783][Normal] SUMMARY, CONCLUSION, AND RECOMMENDATIONS
[P0784][Normal] This chapter presents the summary of findings, conclusions, and recommendations based on the development and evaluation of the Customer Relationship Management System for MJJD TELECOM. It discusses how the developed system addressed the limitations of the existing operational methods used in managing customer applications, installation activities, customer records, and service concerns. The system was designed to improve the organization, accessibility, monitoring, and communication of service-related processes through a centralized web-based platform. The evaluation results, conclusions, and recommendations are presented to determine the overall functionality, efficiency, usability, reliability, and effectiveness of the developed system in supporting the operations and customer relationship management processes of MJJD TELECOM.
[P0785][Normal] Summary
[P0786][Normal] This study focused on the development of a web-based Customer Relationship Management System for MJJD TELECOM. It aimed to determine the current methods used by the company in its daily operations, identify the challenges associated with these methods, determine the necessary functional requirements and system features, and evaluate the developed system based on the International Organization for Standardization and International Electrotechnical Commission 25010 software quality characteristics. The study addressed problems involving delays in responding to customer concerns, delays in processing customer applications, difficulty in monitoring installation schedules and status updates, and the lack of centralized communication and service tracking.
[P0787][Normal] The study employed a quantitative descriptive-developmental research design and utilized the Agile Software Development Methodology in developing and refining the system. It was conducted at MJJD TELECOM, located at Dumlao Street, Dusoc, Bayambang, Pangasinan. The study involved 64 respondents consisting of 1 administrator, 3 technicians, and 60 customers, while 5 Information Technology experts participated in the system evaluation. Structured survey questionnaires were used to gather the necessary data. Frequency distribution and percentage were used to analyze the current methods, problems encountered, functional requirements, and system features, while the average weighted mean was used to evaluate the developed system.
[P0788][Normal] The findings showed that messaging applications or online platforms ranked first among the current operational methods used by MJJD TELECOM, while delays in responding to customer concerns ranked first among the problems encountered. In terms of functional requirements, live chat or direct customer support ranked first, while real-time status tracking for applications and concerns ranked first among the preferred system features. Based on these findings, the developed system centralized customer applications, customer records, technician assignments, installation monitoring, ticketing, live chat, notifications, and public tracking. The system obtained an overall average weighted mean of 4.3, interpreted as Strongly Agree, indicating that the evaluators generally considered the developed system satisfactory based on the evaluated ISO 25010 software quality characteristics.
[P0789][Normal] Conclusions
[P0790][Normal] Based on the findings of the study, the researchers arrived at the following conclusions:
[P0791][Normal (Web)] MJJD TELECOM used both digital and manual methods in its daily operations. Messaging applications or online platforms ranked first, while paper-based documents, face-to-face coordination, phone calls, and text messages were also used.
[P0792][Normal (Web)] The existing operational methods created challenges in customer communication, application processing, and service monitoring. Delays in responding to customer concerns ranked first among the problems identified, showing the need for a more organized and centralized approach.
[P0793][Normal (Web)] The identified functional requirements emphasized communication, application processing, and tracking. Live chat or direct customer support ranked first, followed by online application submission and public tracking of applications and concerns.
[P0794][Normal (Web)] The identified system features addressed the operational needs of MJJD TELECOM. Real-time status tracking ranked first among the preferred features, while live chat AI assistance, document uploading, application processing, technician assignment, and scheduling supported a more centralized workflow.
[P0795][Normal (Web)] The developed Customer Relationship Management System satisfied the evaluated ISO 25010 software quality characteristics. It obtained an overall average weighted mean of 4.3, interpreted as Strongly Agree, indicating that the evaluators considered the system suitable for supporting the customer relationship and service management processes of MJJD TELECOM.
[P0798][Normal] Recommendations
[P0799][Normal] Based on the findings and conclusions of the study, the researchers recommend the following:
[P0800][List Paragraph] MJJD TELECOM may utilize the developed Customer Relationship Management System as a centralized platform for managing customer applications, records, technician assignments, installation schedules, customer concerns, and service-related communication. This may help reduce dependence on separate messaging applications and paper-based records.
[P0801][List Paragraph] The user interface and navigation may be further refined. Although Usability obtained an average weighted mean of 4.2, some navigation-related indicators received comparatively lower ratings. Improvements may focus on making buttons, menus, and navigation tools more intuitive and convenient.
[P0802][List Paragraph] The reliability of the system should be continuously monitored and improved. Further attention may be given to error handling, system stability, and recovery after minor interruptions to support more dependable operation.
[P0803][List Paragraph] The security features of the system should be regularly maintained and reviewed. Login security, access control, uploaded documents, customer information, and data confidentiality should continue to be protected against unauthorized access.
[P0804][List Paragraph] Regular maintenance and testing should be prioritized. Maintainability obtained an average weighted mean of 4.1, interpreted as Agree, making it the lowest-rated ISO 25010 characteristic. Improvements may focus on system updates, testing, troubleshooting, and future enhancements.
