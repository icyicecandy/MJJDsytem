Abstract:       This study developed a web -based Customer Relationship Management 
System for Mary Joy and Jepsy  Dave Telecom to address difficulties in customer 
application processing, record management, installation coordination, and customer 
support communication. Using a quantitative descriptive -developmental approach and 
Agile Software Development Methodology, the study identified existing operational 
methods, problems, functional requirements, and preferred system features. Messaging 
applications or online platforms ranked first among the existing methods, while delays in 
responding to customer concerns ranked first among the problems encountered. Live chat 
or direct customer support ranked first among the functional requirements, while real-time 
status tracking ranked first among the preferred system features. The developed system 
centralized customer applicati ons, records, technician assignments, installation 
monitoring, ticketing, live chat, notifications, and public tracking. The study supports 
Sustainable Development Goal 9 on Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure. The system 
obtained an overall average weighted mean of 4.3, interpreted as Strongly Agree.

CHAPTER I 
INTRODUCTION 
Background of the Study  
  Internet connectivity had become an essential infrastructure that supported 
communication, education, commerce, and public services worldwide. As dependence on 
digital connectivity continued to grow, Internet Service Providers (ISPs) were expected to 
manage not only network performance but also customer interactions and service processes 
efficiently. The increasing number of internet users placed greater pressure on 
telecommunications companies to maintain organized customer records, respond promptly 
to ser vice concerns, and coordinate operational activities effectively. When customer 
information, service requests, and technical coordination were handled through fragmented 
or manual processes, operational inefficiencies often arose. Studies showed that effec tive 
customer relationship management (CRM) systems could improve organizational 
efficiency by centralizing customer data, facilitating communication, and supporting better 
service delivery within telecommunications organizations (Ali & Ahmad, 2024; Fernando 
et al., 2023). These systems helped service providers organize customer interactions, 
monitor service activities, and improve overall customer satisfaction. 
In the telecommunications industry, service quality and customer relationship 
management played a crucial role in maintaining long -term customer relationships. 
Research emphasized that telecommunications providers needed to adopt structured 
systems to manage customer data, service requests, and operational workflows in order to 
ensure service reliability and responsiveness. For example, in the study “The Impact of 
Electronic Customer Relationship Management Strategies on Customer Loyalty: A 

===== PAGE 17 =====
 
   
  2 
 
 
 
   
 
Mediated Model”  by Magatef et al. (2023), the authors found that electronic customer 
relationship management strategies in telecommunications significantly improved 
customer relationship quality, which in turn positively influenced customer loyalty. Their 
findings supported the idea that digital CRM systems were effective because they improved 
communication and helped service providers manage customer interactions in a more 
organized and responsive manner. Likewise, the study “The Relationship between CRM, 
Knowledge Management, Organization Commitment, Customer Profitability and 
Customer Loyalty in Telecommunication Industry: The Mediating Role of Customer 
Satisfaction and the Moderating Role of Brand Image”  by Gazi et al. (2024) showed that 
effective CRM in the telecommunications industry had a strong positive effect on customer 
satisfaction, profitability, and loyalty. The study further showed that better CRM 
performance contributed both directly and indirectly to stronger customer retention through 
improved customer satisfaction. These findings indicated that when customer data and 
service operations were poorly organized, telecommunications providers could experience 
delayed responses, inconsistent service, and difficulty monitoring performance. However, 
when structured digital CRM approaches were implemented, they could improve service 
coordination, responsiveness, and long-term customer relationships. 
In the Philippines, the demand for reliable internet services had increased 
significantly because of the rapid expansion of digital communication, online learning, 
remote work, and electronic commerce. National digitalization initiatives further 
emphasized the importance of expanding internet access and improving service quality 
across the country. According to the Philippine Development Plan 2023 –2028, digital

transformation played a vital role in improving economic productivity and expanding 
access to services (National Economic and Development Authority [NEDA], 2023). 
Similarly, the Philippine Statistics Authority (2023) reported the continued growth of the 
digital economy as internet usage expanded across sectors. These developments 
highlighted the growing importance of internet service providers in supporting the 
country’s digital infrastructure. As internet connectivity became more integrated into 
everyday l ife, customers increasingly expected reliable service, prompt responses to 
concerns, and organized communication with service providers. 
Despite these developments, many internet service providers in the Philippines still 
faced operational challenges related to service management and customer interaction. 
Studies in the Philippine telecommunications sector indicated that service reliability , 
responsiveness, and effective communication significantly influenced how customers 
evaluated their service providers. However, smaller and provincial ISPs often operated 
with limited administrative resources and continued to rely on semi-manual methods such 
as spreadsheets, messaging platforms, and paper -based documentation to manage daily 
operations. Although these methods could be manageable on a small scale, they often 
resulted in fragmented information management as the number of customers increased. 
When customer data, service requests, and operational coordination were handled across 
multiple platforms, it became more difficult to maintain consistent records, monitor service 
activities, and respond to customer concerns in a timely manner. 
This situation was particularly relevant to small provincial internet service 
providers that served local communities without integrated information systems. Many of

these providers managed customer applications, maintained subscriber records, 
coordinated installations, and addressed service concerns through separate communication 
channels and manually maintained files. As operations expanded, these practices could 
lead to incomplete documentation, difficulty retrieving customer records, delayed 
responses to service concerns, and challenges in coordinating operational tasks among 
staff. These issues underscored the need for improved service management practices that 
could support the increasing operational demands of local internet service providers. 
One example of this situation was observed in MJJD TELECOM, a provincial 
internet service provider operating in Bayambang, Pangasinan. As the number of customers 
increased, the company experienced difficulties in organizing customer information, 
monitoring service activities, and coordinating operational tasks. Customer applications 
and inquiries were often received through messaging platforms or physical documents, 
while records were maintained using spreadsheet files and other manual methods. 
Although these approaches enabled the company to continue its operations, they also 
created challenges in maintaining consistent docu mentation, tracking service -related 
activities, and retrieving customer information efficiently. These operational limitations 
could lead to delays in processing requests, inconsistencies in handling service concerns, 
and difficulty maintaining organized records as the company expanded its services. 
Although previous studies examined customer relationship management, service 
quality, and customer satisfaction in telecommunications, most of these studies focused on 
large telecommunications companies or general telecommunications settings. Limited 
literature specifically addressed the operational realities of small provincial internet service

providers, particularly in terms of managing customer applications, installation

Because of this, the specific operational challenges faced by smaller ISPs in provin cial

activities, and improving coordination among operational processes. By centralizing 
information and improving workflow management, the system was expected  to help the 
organization maintain organized records, monitor service activities more effectively, and 
improve communication between the service provider and its customers. 
Conceptual Framework 
  This study was anchored on two complementary recent studies from the 
telecommunications field. The first was the study of Magatef et al. (2023) entitled The 
Impact of Electronic Customer Relationship Management Strategies on Customer Loyalty: 
A Mediated Model. Their study examined electronic customer relationship management (e-
CRM) strategies in the telecommunications sector and explained that e-CRM consisted of 
functional and personal dimensions that influenced customer loyalty through customer 
relationship quality. The study emphasized that structured digital interaction and electronic 
service platforms strengthened the relationship between service providers and customers. 
This was relevant to the present study because the proposed system for MJJD TELECOM

included customer -centered digital functions such as online application submission, 
customer record management, ticketing, live chat, and public status tracking. These 
features reflected the functional dimension of e -CRM because they enabled the company 
to manage customer information and interactions through a centralized web -based 
platform. 
The second anchor was the study of Bandali et al. (2025) entitled ML-Based Fault 
Management Automation in Large-Scale Fixed and Mobile Telecommunication Networks. 
The study discussed how telecommunications service problems were handled through 
trouble tickets and showed that structured ticket -based processing was important for 
improving operational efficiency, resource allocation, and customer satisfaction. It als o 
addressed the prediction of resolution time and dispatch relevance in fault -management 
operations. This study was relevant to the present research because the proposed system 
for MJJD TELECOM included service -related functions such as ticket submission, 
technician assignment, installation monitoring, and service status tracking. Although the 
present study was intended for a small provincial internet service provider rather than a 
large telecommunications operator, the operational principle remained simila r: service 
requests and concerns needed to be properly documented, tracked, assigned, and resolved 
through an organized digital process. 
Guided by these two studies, the present research combined customer relationship 
management and service management into one web -based system for MJJD TELECOM. 
The study of Magatef et al. (2023) supported the customer relationship dimension of the 
framework, particularly digital interaction, organized communication, and centralized

technician assignments, and communication logs in one integrated platform. Through this 
setup, customer information and service activities could be access ed, updated, and 
monitored more efficiently.

Scope and Delimitations 
  This study focused on the design, development, and evaluation of a Customer 
Relationship Management System for MJJD TELECOM (Mary Joy & Jepsy Dave 
Telecom), a provincial internet service provider located in Bayambang , Pangasinan, that 
operated prepaid fiber internet services through Surf2Sawa. The proposed system 
centralized the key operational processes of MJJD TELECOM into a web-based platform, 
particularly customer application processing, installation management, s cheduling, and 
customer communication. 
The system allowed customers to create an account, select a service plan, submit 
an installation application, upload a valid identification document, provide their address, 
and pin their exact location on a map. The administrator reviewed each application,  
manually checked serviceability and slot availability based on the submitted location, and 
either approved or rejected the request. If approved, the administrator assigned a technician 
and scheduled the installation. If rejected, the customer could submit  a new application. 
The system also allowed technicians to view assigned installations, update installation 
statuses, and maintain installation histories. In addition, the system included ticketing, live 
chat, public tracking through reference numbers, and in-system and email notifications that 

===== PAGE 25 =====
 
   
  10 
 
 
 
   
 
informed customers about application approval or rejection, installation schedules, and the 
progress of submitted tickets, including whether they had been resolved or closed. 
The respondents of the study were limited to the administrator, technicians, and 
customers of MJJD TELECOM. The administrator evaluated system functions related to 
application review, technician assignment, scheduling, and records management. 
Technicians assessed installation-related functions, while customers evaluated customer -
facing features such as application submission, ticketing, live chat, notifications, and 
tracking. Data were gathered through structured questionnaires and analyzed using 
descriptive statistics. The developed system was also evaluated based on the ISO/IEC 
25010 software quality characteristics. 
This study was limited to the development and evaluation of the developed system 
and did not include billing, online payment, load purchasing, reload requests, billing 
verification, or direct integration with Surf2Sawa’s internal activation and loading 
platform. The system did not monitor whether customers were active or inactive because 
MJJD TELECOM operated under a prepaid setup in which customers managed their own 
internet loading through authorized retailers after installation and activation. Furthermore, 
the study did not cover physical network infrastructure modification, fiber deployment, 
bandwidth provisioning, or network performance monitoring. The system was intended 
solely for the operational use of MJJD TELECOM and was evaluated based on software  
quality rather than internet speed, connectivity, or

Paradigm of the Study 
  The INPUT block consists of the current methods used by MJJD TELECOM in in 
their daily operations . It also includes the challenges associated with these existing 
methods, as well as the identified functional requirements and system features necessary 
for the development of the proposed Customer Relationship Management System. 
  The PROCESS block illustrates the stages undertaken in the development of the 
proposed system. The study utilizes the Agile Software Development Methodology to 
design, develop, test, and refine the system through iterative cycles. Agile methodology 
allows continuous i mprovement and adaptation based on system requirements. The 
research also applies the Descriptive Developmental Research design to systematically 
develop and evaluate the system. Additionally, the Quantitative Research Method is used 
to assess the develope d system based on the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality 
characteristics, including functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility,

Definition of Terms 
The following terms are defined conceptually and operationally for clarity and 
better understanding of the study: 
Compatibility.  Refers to the degree to which a system can operate with other 
systems or within shared environments without conflict (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, 
compatibility refers to the system functioning properly across commonly used 
browsers/devices for administrators, technicians, and customers. 
Customer Application Processing. Customer application processing refers to the 
structured handling of customer -provided information during service onboarding and 
request management (Benzaid & Taleb, 2022). In this study, it refers to the module where 
customers submit applications online, upload valid IDs, pin installation locations, and 
where administrators review and approve/reject requests. 
Customer Communication. Customer communication refers to organized 
interaction practices that support customer relationship quality and responsiveness in 
service settings (Magatef et al., 2023). In this study, it refers to customer -facing 
communication features such as ticket sub mission and live chat, including logged 
interactions and status updates. 
Customer Relationship Management (CRM). CRM refers to managing customer 
information and interactions in order to improve customer satisfaction and loyalty 
outcomes (Gazi et al., 2024). In this study, CRM refers to the system functions that manage 
customer profiles, application records, service h istory, and interaction logs to support 
organized customer handling. 

===== PAGE 31 =====
 
   
  16 
 
 
 
   
 
Functional Requirements. Functional requirements define what a system must do 
its services, behaviors, and functions needed by users (ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018). In this 
study, it refers to whether the system correctly performs the required tasks such as 
application processing, installation management functions, scheduling, and custom er 
communication. 
Installation Management. Installation management refers to organizing and 
monitoring deployment activities to ensure completion and documentation of service 
installation tasks (Benzaid & Taleb, 2022). In this study, it refers to technician assignment, 
scheduling, status updates, and proof-of-installation uploads handled through the system. 
ISO/IEC 25010. ISO/IEC 25010 provides a product quality model defining 
characteristics for software quality evaluation (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, it is the 
standard used to evaluate the developed system. 
Maintainability. Maintainability refers to how effectively a system can be 
modified to correct faults, improve, or adapt (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, it refers to how 
easily the system can be updated or improved without major redevelopment. 
Performance Efficiency. Performance efficiency refers to system performance 
relative to resources used under stated conditions (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, it refers 
to response time and processing speed when users submit applications, upload files, or 
access tracking pages. 
Portability. Portability refers to how effectively a system can be transferred from 
one environment to another (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, it refers to the system’s ability 
to be deployed across different machines/hosting environments. 

===== PAGE 32 =====
 
   
  17 
 
 
 
   
 
Reliability. Reliability refers to the ability of a system to perform specified 
functions under specified conditions for a specified period (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, 
it refers to stable system operation during key processes such as approvals, updates, and 
record saving. 
Security. Security refers to protecting information so access is appropriate to 
authorization (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, it refers to authenticated access and protection 
of uploaded IDs, application documents and customer records. 
System Features. System features refer to functions and capabilities provided to 
users to meet requirements and support tasks (ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018). In this study, 
system features refer to the modules implemented in the platform (application portal, admin 
dashboard, technician updates, scheduling, ticketing/chat, tracking). 
Usability. Usability refers to the degree to which specified users can use a system 
to achieve goals effectively, efficiently, and with satisfaction (ISO/IEC, 2023).In  this 
study, it refers to ease of use for admins, technicians, and customers when navigating and 
completing tasks.

Research Design  
 This study employed a quantitative descriptive -developmental research design in 
the development and evaluation of the Customer Relationship Management System for 
MJJD TELECOM. 
The quantitative approach was applied in this study to collect measurable and 
objective data from respondents through structured questionnaires. This approach allowed 
the researchers to quantify responses related to system usability, functionality, and 
performance. It was appropriate for the study because it enabled the researchers to 
determine the current operational methods of MJJD TELECOM, identify the problems 
encountered in handling applications, installation activities, scheduling, and customer 
inquiries, and evaluate the developed system based on the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality 
characteristics. 
The study also adopted the descriptive -developmental method. The descriptive 
method was used to describe the existing operational processes, problems encountered, and

  25 
 
 
 
   
 
system requirements of MJJD TELECOM, while the developmental method focused on 
the design and creation of the system based on the identified needs. This method was

actual operational requirements. 
For the system development process, the study utilized the Agile Software 
Development Methodology. Agile was an iterative and flexible approach that allowed 
continuous development and improvement of the system through repeated cycles. Each 
cycle included requirement gathering, planning, design, development, testing, evaluation, 
and refinement. In this study, development was conducted in three-week iterations to allow 
regular review and modification based on feedback and identified issues. This 
methodology w as suitable because it provided a structured yet adaptable process that 
enabled the system to evolve according to the actual needs of MJJD TELECOM.

Agile Software Development Methodology 
The development of the Customer Relationship Management System for MJJD 
TELECOM followed the Agile Software Development Methodology. Agile emphasizes 
iterative development, allowing software functionalities to be planned, developed, 
reviewed, and improved through repeated development cycles. Pasuksmit, Thongtanunam, 
and Karunasekera (2022) explained that Agile teams plan, develop, and deliver software 
increments through time -boxed iterations, allowing changes and refinements to be 
addressed throughout development.  
For the proposed system, Agile enabled the developers to progressively develop 
and refine customer application processing, technician assignment, installation monitoring, 
ticketing, live chat, notifications, and status-tracking functions. The development activities 
consisted of Requirement Analysis, Design, Implementation, Testing, Deployment, and 
Operation and Maintenance, which were performed iteratively as system requirements and 
feedback were addressed. 
Requirement Analysis 
Requirement Analysis involved identifying and documenting the needs of MJJD 
TELECOM and its intended system users. Franch et al. (2023) described requirements 
specification as a core activity of requirements engineering because it documents the 
requirements established during the early stages of software development and supports 
communication among project stakeholders.  
During this phase, the developers gathered information regarding the current 
methods, problems encountered, functional requirements, and desired system features. The

applications, customer record management, installation scheduling, ticketing, live chat, and 
status tracking.

Computer Society in 2024, recognizes software design and software architecture as 
established areas of software engineering concerned with defining the structure and 
organization needed to implement software systems.  
During this phase, the developers prepared the database structure, system 
workflows, interface layouts, user roles, and relationships among the different modules. 
The system was designed around three main users —Administrator, Technician, and 
Customer—with appropriate interfaces and functions provided for each role.

CHAPTER IV 
SUMMARY, CONCLUSION, AND RECOMMENDATIONS 
This chapter presents the summary of findings, conclusions, and recommendations 
based on the development and evaluation of the Customer Relationship Management 
System for MJJD TELECOM. It discusses how the developed system addressed the 
limitations of the existing operational methods used in managing customer applications, 
installation activities, customer records, and service concerns. The system was designed to 
improve the organization, accessibility, monitoring, and communication of service-related 
processes through a centralized web -based platform. The evaluation results, conclusions, 
and recommendations are presented to determine the overall functionality, efficiency, 
usability, reliability, and effectiveness of the developed system in supporting the operations 
and customer relationship management processes of MJJD TELECOM. 
Summary 
 This study focused on the development of a web -based Customer Relationship 
Management System for MJJD TELECOM. It aimed to determine the current methods 
used by the company in its daily operations, identify the challenges associated with these 
methods, det ermine the necessary functional requirements and system features, and 
evaluate the developed system based on the International Organization for Standardization 
and International Electrotechnical Commission 25010 software quality characteristics. The 
study addressed problems involving delays in responding to customer concerns, delays in 
processing customer applications, difficulty in monitoring installation schedules and status 
updates, and the lack of centralized communication and service tracking.  

===== PAGE 81 =====
 
   
  66 
 
 
 
   
 
The study employed a quantitative descriptive -developmental research design and 
utilized the Agile Software Development Methodology in developing and refining the 
system. It was conducted at MJJD TELECOM, located at Dumlao Street, Dusoc, 
Bayambang, Pangasinan. The study involved 64 respondents consisting of 1 administrator, 
3 technicians, and 60 customers, while 5 Information Technology experts participated in 
the system evaluation. Structured survey questionnaires were used to gather the necessary 
data. Frequency distribution and percentage were used to analyze the current methods, 
problems encountered, functional requirements, and system features, while the average 
weighted mean was used to evaluate the developed system.  
The findings showed that messaging applications or online platforms ranked first 
among the current operational methods used by MJJD TELECOM, while delays in 
responding to customer concerns ranked first among the problems encountered. In terms 
of functional requirements, live chat or direct customer support ranked first, while real -
time status tracking for applications and concerns ranked first among the preferred system 
features. Based on these findings, the developed system centralized customer applications, 
customer records, technician assignments, installation monitoring, ticketing, live chat, 
notifications, and public tracking. The system obtained an overall average weighted mean 
of 4.3, interpreted as Strongly Agree, indicating that the evaluators gener ally considered 
the developed system satisfactory based on the evaluated ISO 25010 software quality 
characteristics. 
Conclusions 
Based on the findings of the study, the researchers arrived at the following conclusions: 

===== PAGE 82 =====
 
   
  67 
 
 
 
   
 
1. MJJD TELECOM used both digital and manual methods in its daily operations.  
Messaging applications or online platforms ranked first, while paper -based 
documents, face -to-face coordination, phone calls, and text messages were also 
used. 
2. The existing operational methods created challenges in customer communication, 
application processing, and service monitoring . Delays in responding to customer 
concerns ranked first among the problems identified, showing the need for a more 
organized and centralized approach. 
3. The identified functional requirements emphasized communication, application 
processing, and tracking. Live chat or direct customer support ranked first, followed 
by online application submission and public tracking of applications and concerns. 
4. The identified system features addressed the operational needs of MJJD 
TELECOM. Real-time status tracking ranked first among the preferred features, 
while live chat AI assistance, document uploading, application processing, 
technician assignment, and scheduling supported a more centralized workflow. 
5. The developed Customer Relationship Management System satisfied the evaluated 
ISO 25010 software quality characteristics. It obtained an overall average weighted 
mean of 4.3, interpreted as Strongly Agree , indicating that the evaluators 
considered the system suitable for supporting the customer relationship and service 
management processes of MJJD TELECOM.

Recommendations 
Based on the findings and conclusions of the study, the researchers recommend the 
following: 
1. MJJD TELECOM may utilize the developed Customer Relationship Management 
System as a centralized platform for managing customer applications, records, 
technician assignments, installation schedules, customer concerns, and service -
related communication. This may help reduce dependence on separate messaging 
applications and paper-based records. 
2. The user interface and navigation may be further refined. Although Usability 
obtained an average weighted mean of 4.2, some navigation -related indicators 
received comparatively lower ratings. Improvements may focus on making buttons, 
menus, and navigation tools more intuitive and convenient. 
3. The reliability of the system should be continuously monitored and improved. 
Further attention may be given to error handling, system stability, and recovery 
after minor interruptions to support more dependable operation. 
4. The security features of the system should be regularly maintained and reviewed. 
Login security, access control, uploaded documents, customer information, and 
data confidentiality should continue to be protected against unauthorized access. 
5. Regular maintenance and testing should be prioritized. Maintainability obtained an 
average weighted mean of 4.1, interpreted as Agree, making it the lowest-rated ISO 
25010 characteristic. Improvements may focus on system updates, testing, 
troubleshooting, and future enhancements.

6. Administrators and technicians should be properly oriented on the use of the 
system. User guidance may help ensure that application processing, technician 
assignment, installation updates, ticket management, and customer communication 
are handled consistently. 
7. Future researchers and developers may expand the system based on future 
operational needs. Possible enhancements may include billing, online payment, 
load purchasing, external platform integration, or network-performance monitoring 
if these features become necessary and technically feasible.
