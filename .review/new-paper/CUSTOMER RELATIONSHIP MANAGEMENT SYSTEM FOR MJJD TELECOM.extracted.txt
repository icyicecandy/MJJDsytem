77	Abstract:    This study developed a web-based Customer Relationship Management System for Mary Joy and Jepsy Dave Telecom to address difficulties in customer application processing, record management, installation coordination, and customer support communication. Using a quantitative descriptive-developmental approach and Agile Software Development Methodology, the study identified existing operational methods, problems, functional requirements, and preferred system features. Messaging applications or online platforms ranked first among the existing methods, while delays in responding to customer concerns ranked first among the problems encountered. Live chat or direct customer support ranked first among the functional requirements, while real-time status tracking ranked first among the preferred system features. The developed system centralized customer applications, records, technician assignments, installation monitoring, ticketing, live chat, notifications, and public tracking. The study supports Sustainable Development Goal 9 on Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure. The system obtained an overall average weighted mean of 4.3, interpreted as Strongly Agree.

255	CHAPTER I
256	INTRODUCTION
257	Background of the Study
258	Internet connectivity has become an essential infrastructure supporting communication, education, commerce, employment, and access to digital services. As dependence on internet-based services continues to increase, Internet Service Providers are expected not only to provide connectivity but also to manage customer information, service requests, and customer communication efficiently. In the telecommunications industry, organized customer relationship management has become increasingly important because effective management of customer information and interactions can contribute to improved service delivery, customer satisfaction, and long-term customer relationships (Magatef et al., 2023; Gazi et al., 2024).
259	Customer relationship management is particularly important in telecommunications because providers handle numerous customer-related activities, including applications, service requests, records, technical concerns, and follow-up communication. Magatef et al. (2023) found that electronic customer relationship management strategies improved customer relationship quality and contributed to customer loyalty in the telecommunications sector. Similarly, Gazi et al. (2024) found that customer relationship management was associated with customer satisfaction, profitability, and loyalty. These findings demonstrate the importance of structured digital systems that allow telecommunications providers to organize customer information and manage service-related interactions more effectively.
260	In the Philippines, digital transformation has increased the importance of reliable and accessible digital services. The Philippine Development Plan 2023–2028 emphasizes digital transformation as part of the country’s economic and social development agenda. The Philippine Statistics Authority also reported the continued contribution and growth of the digital economy, with telecommunications services representing a major component of digital-enabling infrastructure (National Economic and Development Authority, 2023; Philippine Statistics Authority, 2024). These developments highlight the important role of internet service providers in supporting digital activities and the increasing need for organized customer and service management. Philippine Development Plan
261	In the Philippine internet-service setting, customer service, responsiveness, and service quality influence the experiences and satisfaction of subscribers. Paulino and Esteban (2023) identified customer service and after-sales support as relevant attributes among Filipino internet subscribers, while Saflor et al. (2024) found that responsiveness and data privacy influenced perceived service quality among internet service users in a Philippine provincial setting. These findings suggest that effective service delivery requires not only reliable connectivity but also organized communication, documentation, and customer-support processes.
262	For smaller and provincial internet service providers, managing customer applications, records, installation activities, and service concerns through separate manual and digital channels can create operational difficulties. When information is distributed among messaging applications, paper documents, spreadsheets, phone calls, and face-to-face communication, maintaining consistent records and monitoring the progress of customer requests can become more difficult. This creates a need for centralized systems that can support organized information management and coordinated service processes.
263	This situation was evident in MJJD TELECOM, a provincial internet service provider operating in Bayambang, Pangasinan. The company handled customer applications, customer records, installation coordination, and customer concerns using a combination of digital and manual methods. As its operations involved information distributed across different channels, the company experienced challenges in organizing customer information, monitoring applications and installations, coordinating service activities, and responding to customer concerns. These operational conditions demonstrated the need for a more centralized approach to managing customer and service-related information.
264	Although previous studies have examined customer relationship management, customer satisfaction, service quality, technician scheduling, and support-ticket management in telecommunications, much of the existing literature focuses on larger telecommunications environments or examines these operational areas separately. Limited literature has addressed the development of an integrated and localized platform that combines customer application processing, customer-record management, installation coordination, technician assignment, service tracking, ticketing, and customer communication for a small provincial internet service provider. This identified gap provided the basis for developing a system specifically suited to the operational requirements of MJJD TELECOM.
265	To address these concerns, the study developed a web-based Customer Relationship Management System for MJJD TELECOM. The system provides a centralized platform for managing customer applications and records, coordinating installation activities and technician assignments, handling support tickets and live-chat communication, providing notifications, and enabling application and ticket tracking. Through the integration of these functions, the system was designed to provide a more organized approach to customer relationship and service management within MJJD TELECOM.
266	Conceptual Framework
267	This study was anchored on two complementary studies from the telecommunications field that supported the customer relationship and service-management aspects of the developed system.
268	The first anchor was the study of Magatef et al. (2023), which examined electronic customer relationship management in the telecommunications sector. The study emphasized that structured digital interaction and electronic service platforms can strengthen customer relationship quality and support customer loyalty. This was relevant to the present study because the Customer Relationship Management System for MJJD TELECOM included customer-centered functions such as online application submission, customer record management, ticketing, live chat, notifications, and public status tracking. These functions supported more organized communication and interaction between the company and its customers.
269	The second anchor was the study of Bandali et al. (2025), which discussed structured fault and ticket management in telecommunications operations. The study highlighted the importance of properly documenting, assigning, monitoring, and resolving service-related concerns. This was relevant to the present study because the developed system included functions such as support-ticket management, technician assignment, installation monitoring, service-status updates, and tracking of customer concerns.
270	Guided by these two studies, the present research integrated customer relationship management and service-management processes into one centralized web-based system. The customer relationship perspective supported the organization of customer information and communication, while the service-management perspective supported the structured handling of applications, installations, technician assignments, tickets, and service concerns. These combined concepts guided the design of the Customer Relationship Management System for MJJD TELECOM.

295	Scope and Delimitations
296	This study focused on the design, development, and evaluation of a Customer Relationship Management System for MJJD TELECOM (Mary Joy & Jepsy Dave Telecom), a provincial internet service provider located in Bayambang, Pangasinan, that operated prepaid fiber internet services through Surf2Sawa. The system centralized the key operational processes of MJJD TELECOM into a web-based platform, particularly customer application processing, installation management, scheduling, and customer communication.
297	The system allowed customers to create an account, select a service plan, submit an installation application, upload the required photographs of a valid identification document, provide their address, and pin their exact location on a map. The administrator reviewed each application, manually checked serviceability based on the submitted location, reviewed technician and schedule availability, and either approved or rejected the request. If approved, the administrator assigned a technician and scheduled the installation. If rejected, the customer could submit a new application. Technicians could view assigned installations, update installation statuses, and maintain installation histories. The system also included ticketing, live chat, public tracking through reference numbers, and in-system and email notifications regarding application decisions, installation schedules, and ticket progress. In addition, the system implemented role-based access control and customer email verification. Google authentication was available only to customers, while administrators and technicians used staff credentials and mandatory authenticator-based two-factor authentication.
298	The respondents of the study were limited to the administrator, technicians, and customers of MJJD TELECOM. The administrator evaluated system functions related to application review, technician assignment, scheduling, and records management. Technicians assessed installation-related functions, while customers evaluated customer-facing features such as application submission, ticketing, live chat, notifications, and tracking. Data were gathered through structured questionnaires and analyzed using descriptive statistics. The developed system was also evaluated based on the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality characteristics.
299	This study was limited to the development and evaluation of the system and did not include billing, online payment, load purchasing, reload requests, billing verification, or direct integration with Surf2Sawa’s internal activation and loading platform. The system did not monitor real-time internet connectivity, prepaid-load status, subscription activity, or whether an installed internet service was currently online. Customer and technician accounts could be enabled or disabled by the administrator solely for system access control and did not indicate the status of the customer’s internet connection or subscription. Furthermore, the study did not cover physical network infrastructure modification, fiber deployment, bandwidth provisioning, or network performance monitoring. The system was intended solely for the operational use of MJJD TELECOM and was evaluated based on software quality rather than internet speed, connection availability, or network service performance.

315	Definition of Terms
316	The following terms are defined conceptually and operationally for clarity and better understanding of the study:
317	Compatibility. Refers to the degree to which a system can operate with other systems or within shared environments without conflict (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, compatibility refers to the system’s ability to function properly across commonly used browsers and devices for administrators, technicians, and customers.
318	Customer Application Processing. Customer application processing refers to the structured handling of customer-provided information during service onboarding and request management (Benzaid & Taleb, 2022). In this study, it refers to the module where customers submit applications online, upload valid IDs, pin installation locations, and where administrators review and approve or reject requests.
319	Customer Communication. Customer communication refers to organized interaction practices that support customer relationship quality and responsiveness in service settings (Magatef et al., 2023). In this study, it refers to customer-facing communication features such as ticket submission and live chat, including logged interactions and status updates.
320	Customer Relationship Management (CRM). CRM refers to managing customer information and interactions to improve customer satisfaction and loyalty outcomes (Gazi et al., 2024). In this study, CRM refers to the system functions that manage customer profiles, application records, service history, and interaction logs to support organized customer handling.
321	Functional Requirements. Functional requirements define what a system must do, including the services, behaviors, and functions needed by users (ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018). In this study, they refer to the required system tasks, such as application processing, installation management, scheduling, and customer communication.
322	Installation Management. Installation management refers to organizing and monitoring deployment activities to ensure the completion and documentation of service installation tasks (Benzaid & Taleb, 2022). In this study, it refers to technician assignment, scheduling, status updates, and proof-of-installation uploads handled through the system.
323	ISO/IEC 25010. ISO/IEC 25010 provides a product quality model that defines characteristics for software quality evaluation (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, it refers to the standard used to evaluate the developed system.
324	Maintainability. Maintainability refers to how effectively a system can be modified to correct faults, improve, or adapt (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, it refers to how easily the system can be updated or improved without major redevelopment.
325	Performance Efficiency. Performance efficiency refers to system performance relative to the resources used under stated conditions (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, it refers to response time and processing speed when users submit applications, upload files, or access tracking pages.
326	Portability. Portability refers to how effectively a system can be transferred from one environment to another (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, it refers to the system’s ability to be deployed across different machines and hosting environments.
327	Reliability. Reliability refers to the ability of a system to perform specified functions under specified conditions for a specified period (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, it refers to stable system operation during key processes such as approvals, updates, and record saving.
328	Security. Security refers to protecting information so that access is appropriate according to authorization (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, it refers to authenticated access and the protection of uploaded IDs, application documents, and customer records.
329	System Features. System features refer to the functions and capabilities provided to users to meet requirements and support tasks (ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018). In this study, system features refer to the modules implemented in the platform, including the application portal, administrator dashboard, technician updates, scheduling, ticketing and chat, and tracking.
330	Usability. Usability refers to the degree to which specified users can use a system to achieve goals effectively, efficiently, and with satisfaction (ISO/IEC, 2023). In this study, it refers to the ease of use for administrators, technicians, and customers when navigating the system and completing tasks.

333	Foreign Literature
334	Electronic customer relationship management had been shown to play an important role in the telecommunications industry. In the study “The Impact of Electronic Customer Relationship Management Strategies on Customer Loyalty: A Mediated Model” by Magatef et al. 350	Research Design
351	This study employed a quantitative descriptive-developmental research design in the development and evaluation of the Customer Relationship Management System for MJJD TELECOM.
352	The quantitative approach was applied to collect measurable and objective data from respondents through structured questionnaires. This approach allowed the researchers to quantify responses related to system usability, functionality, and performance. It was appropriate for the study because it enabled the researchers to determine the current operational methods of MJJD TELECOM, identify the problems encountered in handling applications, installation activities, scheduling, and customer inquiries, and evaluate the developed system based on the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality characteristics.
353	The study also adopted the descriptive-developmental method. The descriptive method was used to describe the existing operational processes, problems encountered, and system requirements of MJJD TELECOM, while the developmental method focused on the design and creation of the system based on the identified needs. This method was appropriate because it allowed the researchers to analyze the existing situation and develop a solution that addressed the identified problems, ensuring that the system was based on actual operational requirements.
354	For the system development process, the study utilized the Agile Software Development Methodology. Agile is an iterative and flexible approach that allows continuous development and improvement of the system through repeated cycles. Each cycle included requirement gathering, planning, design, development, testing, evaluation, and refinement. In this study, development was conducted in three-week iterations to allow regular review and modification based on feedback and identified issues. This methodology was suitable because it provided a structured yet adaptable process that enabled the system to evolve according to the actual needs of MJJD TELECOM.
355	Population and Locale of the Study
356	The study was conducted at MJJD TELECOM (Mary Joy & Jepsy Dave Telecom), located at Dumlao St., Dusoc, Bayambang, Pangasinan. MJJD TELECOM is a provincial internet service provider that offers prepaid fiber internet services through its partnership with Surf2Sawa. The company manages customer applications, installation scheduling, customer records, and customer service concerns within its service coverage area. The locale of the study was selected because the organization experienced operational challenges in managing customer applications, installation coordination, and customer communication using semi-manual methods.
357	The respondents of the study consisted of individuals directly involved in the operational processes of MJJD TELECOM, particularly the administrator, technicians, and customers of the internet service. The administrator and technicians were included because they were directly responsible for reviewing customer applications, coordinating installation activities, maintaining customer records, scheduling service tasks, and addressing customer concerns. Customers were included because they had direct experience with the company’s service processes, such as application submission, installation coordination, and customer support communication.
358	The study involved a total of 64 respondents, composed of 1 administrator, 3 technicians, and 60 customers. Purposive sampling was used to select the customer respondents based on their actual experience with MJJD TELECOM and its service processes. Meanwhile, total enumeration was applied to the administrator and technicians because of their limited number within the organization. Total enumeration involved including all members of a specific group or population in the study to ensure complete representation of the identified respondents (QuestionPro, 2023).
359	The developed system was evaluated by 6 evaluators, consisting of 5 Information Technology (IT) experts and 1 client representative who served as the system administrator. The IT experts were selected through purposive sampling based on their knowledge and experience in system development, software evaluation, or related fields. The administrator was included as the intended primary operator of the system and evaluated its suitability for the actual operational processes of MJJD TELECOM. Their combined technical and operational perspectives helped provide a credible and relevant assessment of the developed system based on the selected ISO/IEC 25010 software quality characteristics. Purposive sampling is a non-probability sampling technique in which respondents are intentionally selected based on their knowledge, experience, and relevance to the objectives of the study (Nikolopoulou, 2023).

Administrators can manage customer and technician accounts, review applications and identification documents, assess serviceability, approve or reject applications, schedule installations, assign technicians, monitor support requests, maintain records, review system logs, and generate operational reports. Technicians can access assigned installations, view relevant customer information, update installation statuses, upload installation documentation, and respond to assigned support concerns.

1080	Technical Design
1081	The Customer Relationship Management System for MJJD TELECOM was designed as a centralized and responsive web-based platform that can be accessed through desktop and mobile web browsers. The system was developed using the Laravel framework and uses a MySQL-compatible relational database to store and manage customer accounts, applications, installation records, support tickets, conversations, notifications, authentication records, and system activity logs.
1082	The system follows a role-based design that provides administrators, technicians, and customers with access to functions appropriate to their responsibilities. Administrators can manage customer and technician accounts, review applications and identification documents, assign technicians, schedule and monitor installations, manage support tickets and live-chat conversations, view operational analytics, generate printable reports, and review system logs. Technicians can view relevant customer records, manage assigned installation work orders, upload installation documentation, update installation progress, and handle assigned support concerns. Customers can submit and track applications, view installation progress, create and monitor support tickets, communicate through live chat, and receive system and email notifications.
1083	The system uses a centralized database to maintain consistency across its modules. Updates made during application processing, technician assignment, installation work, or ticket handling are reflected in the appropriate administrator, technician, and customer interfaces. Public tracking functions also allow customers to check application and ticket progress using their assigned reference numbers.
1084	Security controls include role-based access authorization, password protection, customer email verification, mandatory additional authentication for administrator and technician accounts, recovery codes, trusted-browser management, account enablement and disablement, and the recording of relevant authentication and operational activities in the system logs. These controls help restrict access to customer information and uploaded documents to authorized users.
1085	System Design
1086	The Customer Relationship Management System for MJJD TELECOM was developed using interconnected modules that represent the primary functions performed by its customers, administrator, and technicians. These modules share a centralized database so that updates made in one operational area are reflected in the relevant interfaces and records.
1087	The Customer Interface Module allows customers to register and sign in, submit service applications, provide applicant information, select an internet service plan, upload identification documents, specify an installation address, and pin the service location on a map. Customers can also monitor application and installation progress, submit and track support tickets, communicate through live chat, view notifications, and request application cancellation when applicable.
1088	The Administrator Interface Module provides centralized management of customer applications, customer records, installation activities, technician accounts, support tickets, and live-chat conversations. The administrator can review submitted applications and identification documents, approve or reject applications, assign technicians, schedule or reschedule installations, monitor installation progress, manage customer and technician account access, assign support concerns, and oversee customer communication. This module also provides operational analytics, printable reports, and system logs for monitoring system and user activities.
1089	The Technician Interface Module enables technicians to view relevant customer records, assigned installation jobs, and assigned support tickets. Technicians can access installation work orders and service-location information, update installation progress, enter technical installation details, upload documentation photographs, complete and activate assigned installations, review support concerns, and communicate with customers through routed live-chat conversations.
1090	The Public Tracking and Notification Module allows users to track submitted applications and support tickets using their assigned reference or tracking numbers without signing in. In-system and email notifications inform customers about important events, including application decisions, installation schedules and updates, and ticket progress or resolution.
1091	Access to these modules is controlled according to each user’s assigned role. Customer accounts use email verification, while administrator and technician accounts use staff authentication with additional security safeguards. The modules and their functions are represented in the developed system interfaces and documented operational workflows.

1156	Summary
1157	This study focused on the development of a web-based Customer Relationship Management System for MJJD TELECOM. It aimed to determine the current methods used by the company in its daily operations, identify the challenges associated with these methods, determine the necessary functional requirements and system features, and evaluate the developed system based on the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality characteristics. The study addressed problems involving delays in responding to customer concerns, delays in processing customer applications, difficulty in monitoring installation schedules and status updates, and the lack of centralized communication and service tracking.
1158	The study employed a quantitative descriptive-developmental research design and utilized the Agile Software Development Methodology in developing and refining the system. It was conducted at MJJD TELECOM, located at Dumlao Street, Dusoc, Bayambang, Pangasinan. The study involved 64 respondents, consisting of 1 administrator, 3 technicians, and 60 customers. The developed system was evaluated by 6 evaluators, consisting of 5 Information Technology experts and 1 client representative who served as the system administrator. Structured survey questionnaires were used to gather the necessary data. Frequency distribution and percentage were used to analyze the current methods, problems encountered, functional requirements, and system features, while the average weighted mean was used to evaluate the developed system.
1159	The findings showed that messaging applications or online platforms ranked first among the current operational methods used by MJJD TELECOM, while delays in responding to customer concerns ranked first among the problems encountered. In terms of functional requirements, live chat or direct customer support ranked first, while real-time status tracking for applications and concerns ranked first among the preferred system features. Based on these findings, the developed system centralized customer applications, customer records, technician assignments, installation monitoring, ticketing, live chat, notifications, and public tracking. The system obtained an overall average weighted mean of 4.3, interpreted as Strongly Agree, indicating that the evaluators generally considered the developed system satisfactory based on the evaluated ISO/IEC 25010 software quality characteristics.
1160	Conclusions
1161	Based on the findings of the study, the researchers arrived at the following conclusions:
1162	MJJD TELECOM used both digital and manual methods in its daily operations. Messaging applications or online platforms ranked first, while paper-based documents, face-to-face coordination, phone calls, and text messages were also used.
1163	The existing operational methods created challenges in customer communication, application processing, and service monitoring. Delays in responding to customer concerns ranked first among the problems identified, showing the need for a more organized and centralized approach.
1164	The identified functional requirements emphasized communication, application processing, and tracking. Live chat or direct customer support ranked first, followed by online application submission and public tracking of applications and concerns.
1165	The identified system features addressed the operational needs of MJJD TELECOM. Real-time status tracking ranked first among the preferred features, while live chat AI assistance, document uploading, application processing, technician assignment, and scheduling supported a more centralized workflow.
1166	The developed Customer Relationship Management System was positively evaluated based on the selected ISO/IEC 25010 software quality characteristics. It obtained an overall average weighted mean of 4.3, interpreted as Strongly Agree, indicating that the evaluators considered the system suitable for supporting the customer relationship and service-management processes of MJJD TELECOM.
1167	Recommendations
1168	Based on the findings and conclusions of the study, the researchers recommend the following:
1169	MJJD TELECOM may utilize the developed Customer Relationship Management System as a centralized platform for managing customer applications, records, technician assignments, installation schedules, customer concerns, and service-related communication. This may help reduce dependence on separate messaging applications and paper-based records.
1170	The user interface and navigation may be further refined. Although Usability obtained an average weighted mean of 4.2, some navigation-related indicators received comparatively lower ratings. Improvements may focus on making buttons, menus, and navigation tools more intuitive and convenient.
1171	The reliability of the system should be continuously monitored and improved. Further attention may be given to error handling, system stability, and recovery after minor interruptions to support more dependable operation.
1172	The security features of the system should be regularly maintained and reviewed. Login security, access control, uploaded documents, customer information, and data confidentiality should continue to be protected against unauthorized access.
1173	Regular maintenance and testing should be prioritized. Maintainability obtained an average weighted mean of 4.1, interpreted as Agree, making it the lowest-rated ISO/IEC 25010 characteristic. Improvements may focus on system updates, testing, troubleshooting, and future enhancements.
1174	Administrators and technicians should be properly oriented on the use of the system. User guidance may help ensure that application processing, technician assignment, installation updates, ticket management, and customer communication are handled consistently.
1175	Future researchers and developers may expand the system based on future operational needs. Possible enhancements may include billing, online payment, load purchasing, external platform integration, or network-performance monitoring if these features become necessary and technically feasible.
